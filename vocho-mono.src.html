The affectionate shorthand born from decades of the Beetle’s ubiquity on Mexican roads, where it was manufactured until 2003 — longer than anywhere else in the world.

The premise

Mexico's most beloved car never really left.

The Beetle rolled off the line at Puebla in 1964 and became something its engineers never drew: a piece of Mexican identity.

Shortened to vocho, it carried families, taxi fleets, and six decades of street life. It was the car people learned to drive in, the car that got fixed on the kerb, the car that was still on every corner long after the rest of the world had moved on. Production only stopped in 2003, and even then it stopped in Puebla, not anywhere else.

Nobody has rebuilt it to the standard the car deserves — real craft, real engineering, and a story that could only come from here. So we are building it: one at a time, in the Bajío, with trades that have been here longer than the car has.

The name

A German car, made local by a single word.

Nobody in Mexico agrees on where vocho came from. Some hold it is simply a clipping of Volkswagen. Others say it is VO fused with bicho — bug. The argument has never been settled and probably never will be.

What isn't in question is the affection in it. It is the single most valuable thing in this business, and it costs nothing to inherit.

The absence

A category with no Latin American voice.

ICON, Singer, Everrati and JIA have built serious businesses reviving iconic platforms with modern engineering. Every one of them is based in the US, the UK or Japan, working on Broncos, 911s and Land Cruisers. None are building the car that means the most to Mexico. None are building in Mexico.

The build

Every panel off. Nothing left to guesswork.

One car at a time, in a single queue. The exterior reads exactly as it should. The platform underneath does not.

I

Strip Despiece

The shell comes off the pan and goes to bare metal. Everything that was ever bolted on is catalogued, tagged, and either restored or replaced. Nothing is painted over.

II

Metal & finish Lámina

Corrected, refinished, and put back true. Chrome re-plated, glass replaced, whitewalls and roof rack correct to the period. The car has to be right before it is interesting.

III

Drivetrain Primero

The pan comes back with a different heart. Our own conversion system — engineered, not adapted — badged and finished to the same standard as the coachwork around it.

IV

Trim Guarnición

Palm, leather, textile, brass. The last stretch is the slowest, and it is the part an owner touches every day. It is done by hand, by people whose families have done it for generations.

The interior

The craft is the argument.

Hand-woven palm on the dash. Saddle leather where vinyl was. Regional weaving set into the seats, from the same trades the Bajío has run for generations. This is where “built in San Miguel” stops being an address and becomes the product itself.

The platform

Primero. Engineered, not adapted.

A purpose-built conversion system, badged and finished to the same standard as the coachwork around it. Real range, real torque, serviceable in-market. Named for its place as the first proprietary system in the house — the spine every future platform will share.

The exterior looks backward. Everything underneath is pointed the other way.

1

Inverter

The commission

Low volume, by design.

Scarcity and finish standard set the price, not throughput. Every car is spec'd with its owner — colour, hide, textile, powertrain configuration — and photographed through every stage of the build.
